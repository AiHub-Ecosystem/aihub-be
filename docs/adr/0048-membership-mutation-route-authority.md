# ADR-0048: Membership mutation authority is decided per route, before the target

- Status: Accepted
- Related issue: #149
- Amends: ADR-0029

Every Organization management mutation now answers each caller outside its authority with one Safe Authorization Denial per route: no active membership, a disabled membership, a suspended Organization (including one suspended while the request is in flight), or a Membership Role with no authority on that route. Callers who have proved authority keep specific answers about the request itself. This follows the listings, the rename route, and the Organization Audit Read, and it closes one leak those did not have.

The membership routes looked up the target before deciding authority, because the policy needs the target's role. A `member` naming an unknown username therefore got `404`, and naming a real one got `403`. That let any member probe which usernames hold a membership in their Organization, including disabled memberships the Organization Roster deliberately omits. Authority is now decided in two steps. The first needs only the route, the caller's role, and whether the named username is the caller's own: owners and admins may change roles; they, and anyone acting on their own membership, may disable; only owners may transfer. A caller who fails it receives the route's denial whether or not the target exists. The second step is the existing target-level policy, for callers who passed the first. An admin acting on an owner or admin, or an owner transferring to themselves or to another owner, now gets a specific reason instead of one shared "not allowed". An unknown or cross-organization target is still `404` for such callers.

This amends ADR-0029, which made unknown targets `404` for every caller. That remains true for callers with authority on the route. For those without it, the answer is `403`, trading a slightly less precise error for a caller who could not have acted anyway against not disclosing membership existence. The route-level decision is a pure function in the application policy. The repository evaluates it under its locks, where one query reads the caller and target rows together, and answers with the route's denial before anything that depends on whether the target exists can reach the response. The route denials are application constants the repository imports. That keeps the dependency pointing inward and puts the whole rule in one testable place.

The Organization Audit Trail is unchanged. The repository still evaluates the target-level policy for a real target, and records `insufficient_authority` exactly when that policy would have refused before, even though the response now carries the route's denial. That policy checks a role change's or transfer's target is active before it looks at the caller's role. So a member changing a disabled membership's role, or an admin transferring to one, is still not recorded, as before, though the answer is now `403` rather than `404`. A member disabling someone else's disabled membership is recorded, as before. An unknown target still writes nothing, as ADR-0035 requires. API key and invitation-send refusals are still not recorded, because their role refusals happen before any durable act.
